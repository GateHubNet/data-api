# ADR-0020: MPT data: the issuance ID as the asset, state tables with two aggregates, and holders ordered at read time

**Status:** Accepted. Amends [ADR-0010](0010-ripple-v2-wire-compatibility.md#amendment-11-2026-10-31-mptokens) (Amendment 11, items 108 to 117; items 85 and 101 edited in place). Applies
[ADR-0015](0015-historical-account-state-from-clickhouse.md)'s approach (state from the ClickHouse history, never from a node) to MPTokens, [ADR-0016](0016-nft-and-uritoken-data.md)'s coverage rule
(`FeatureCoverageService`) to one more feature, and [ADR-0008](0008-no-aggregate-materialized-views.md)'s rule to its two aggregates. The design is in [the MPT spec](../superpowers/specs/2026-10-17-mpt-data-design.md), program 2 sub-project D9.

## Context
The explorer has a page for an MPT issuance and a tab of them in its token list: the issuance's state, flags and metadata, its holders by balance, what moved, was clawed back or was locked. The index could answer none of it, and it dropped what it did see:

- **MPT payments were not payments.** The payment parser dropped a payment with an MPT amount, and the balance-change parser ignored `MPToken` nodes, so an account's history had holes wherever it held an MPT.
- **Balances are not on one key.** A holder's `MPToken` node and the issuance's node change in different transactions, a field set for the first time is not in `PreviousFields`, and a holder's history is spread over the ledgers it was touched in.
- **Metadata is what the issuer wrote.** XLS-89 recommends short keys, early issuances use long keys or a draft's `urls`, and nothing on the ledger enforces either.
- **Xahau has no MPT**, and the issued tokens' list (D6) already has a `kind` that D9 was to extend.

## Decision
1. **An MPT is its issuance ID.** In every `(currency, issuer)` column and answer, `currency` is the 48 upper-case hex digits of the ID and `issuer` the issuer the ID holds. 48 hex digits are always an MPT; 3-character and 40-hex codes stay issued currencies. Assets are written `<48hex>` or `<48hex>+<issuer>`, and another issuer than the ID's is a 400. So the existing `payments` and `balance_changes` tables take MPT rows with no schema change.
2. **Amounts are the ledger's integer units, never scaled.** They are `UInt64` in the MPT tables, `String` in the existing tables, and **integer strings beside the issuance ID in the MPT routes' answers**. D5's amount object (`{currency, issuer, value, mpt_issuance_id}`) stays what the ledger objects' routes serve, unchanged. The spec's decision 17 said the MPT routes would serve it too; its section 7's examples and E14's client read integer strings, and that is what was built (ruling C25, [ADR-0010](0010-ripple-v2-wire-compatibility.md) item 109). The scale and the ticker of an ID, for a page that shows amounts of several, come from `GET /v2/mpt?ids=`. Scaling is the client's.
3. **Five ledger tables and two aggregates, rebuilt per touched issuance** (migration 017, parser version 10, `MPT_PARSER_VERSION`). `mpt_issuance_states` and `mpt_holder_states` hold every state of a node, ordered by the issuance and by the holder; `mpt_touches` says in ledger order what changed, as `amm_pool_touches`, `nft_touches` and `state_touches` do for their jobs; `mpt_transfers` (by ledger) and `mpt_issuance_transfers` (by issuance) hold what moved. The aggregator's `mpt_state` job rebuilds from them the current `mpt_holders` of the pairs touched, then the `mpt_issuances` row of the issuance, each a whole new row under a newer version, and readers keep the newest row of a key (`LIMIT 1 BY`, never `FINAL`). The holders are written first, so a reader never sees a count ahead of the rows it counts. **`last_ledger` claims every pair touched below it**, so a rebuild first reads, for its IDs, every pair touched above each `last_ledger` up to the head; a retry and the `agg_dirty` hook need nothing else.
4. **Holders are ordered at read time.** A balance is `amount + escrowed_amount`, and the list is the issuance's rows of `mpt_holders`, the newest of each holder, ordered by the balance and then the address. **Keeping a rank or a sorted table** would rewrite an issuance's rows at each transfer; an issuance's holders are thousands at most, and the read is bounded by them. The marker holds the balance as text and the holder, so that a balance above 2^53 pages exactly.
5. **The metadata is decoded leniently, by the job.** The long and the short keys of XLS-89 and a draft's `urls` all read as one set of keys, a field of the wrong type or an unknown key is dropped, and `metadata_conforms` says whether every rule of XLS-89 holds of the decoded object. The hex is always served, so that nothing an issuer wrote is lost. Nothing is checked against the issuer's domain, and URLs are served as written: the explorer decides what it links.
6. **`state_known` says what the index does not know.** An issuance with touched holders but no state row in the coverage (its own node has not changed since the deploy) has `state_known = 0`: it has no numbers to serve, so `/v2/mpt/{issuanceId}` is a 404 and the list and `?ids=` leave it out or bare, while its holders and transfers are served. There is **no node fallback**: the explorer reads the node beside them.
7. **Coverage is the feature `mpt`** of `FeatureCoverageService`, started by the amendment `MPTokensV1` (ledger 99,226,369 on the XRP Ledger; on XRPL Testnet only an indexed `EnableAmendment` counts). **Xahau and Xahau Testnet cannot have MPT** (`NetworkProfile.hasMpt` false): the feature is `unavailable`, so `complete: true`, no route reads an MPT table, and the lists are empty and an issuance a 404.
8. **`kind=mpt` is D6's route.** `TokensController` hands the kind to `MptTokensService` through the `TOKEN_LIST_SERVICES` map, so the envelope, the checks and the marker are D6's, and the MPT row and totals are its own. The holdings of an account read `mpt_holder_states` directly and not the aggregate, so a balance is as of the newest committed ledger and not of the job's last run.

## Consequences
- **A parser version and a whole reprocess.** Parser version 10 writes the five tables and the MPT rows of the existing ones, so the MPT history needs ledger 99,226,369 upward rewritten. The program's one reprocess of the whole history covers it, and D9 asks for no run of its own unless that one is skipped ([backfill](../backfill.md#mpt-data-re-ingest)). Until then an issuance untouched since the deploy has no known state, and `complete` is false.
- **`mpt_state` waits for a stats catch-up.** It runs after `stats_dirty` (job 11 of 17, budget 3 s), so that the jobs before `stats_live` keep to 27 of the 30 s of a tick. During a catch-up it gets no budget: a new issuance waits and `syncer_mpt_state_lag_ledgers` climbs, which the alert excludes. A brand-new issuance answers 404 until the job's next run, about a minute.
- **The balance-change rows read as a trust line's.** Each MPT change is two rows, the holder's and the issuer's mirror, so an issuer's rows sum as an issued token's do. `/analytics/*`, `stats` and `reports` show an MPT line in raw units at value 0, since an MPT has no rate. Exchanges, AMM pools and path payments still skip MPT amounts: MPTokensV2 is not enabled.
- **Reads cost their rows and two granules a part.** `kind=mpt` reads `mpt_issuances` three times (the list, its counts, the IDs of the distinct holders): a scalar array of the IDs kept it at two, and did not answer within the client's 30 s at 100,000 IDs and 800,000 holders, where the subquery takes 14 ms.
- **Retention costs a quiet holder.** The ledger tables are dropped by the P6 and P7 retention, and the aggregates never are. A block dropped from `mpt_holder_states` loses the newest row of a holder unchanged since, so its holdings leave `/v2/accounts/{address}/mpt`, a later rebuild of that pair would delete its `mpt_holders` row, and an issuance whose own node last changed there turns `state_known = 0` at its next rebuild ([operations](../operations.md#retention-of-the-mpt-tables)).
- **The lenient decoding keeps what a stricter one would refuse,** so a `ticker` may be anything a string can be, cut to 16 characters. `metadata_conforms` is the only statement of quality, and a client that shows a ticker as trusted has to read it.
